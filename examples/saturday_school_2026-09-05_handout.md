# The one file that changes every answer you get

You have been typing questions into a chat box and getting answers that sound right.

The answers sound right because the thing on the other end is very good at sounding right. It is
not thinking about you. It cannot — you never told it anything about you.

**This file is how you tell it.** Fill it in once. Paste it in front of your questions from now on.

---

## How to use it

1. Open a plain text file on your own computer. Any text editor. Notepad is fine.
2. Copy everything between the lines below into it.
3. **Fill it in properly.** Twenty minutes. It is boring and it is the entire point.
4. From now on, paste the whole filled-in block in front of any question you ask an assistant.
5. When something changes about your business, change the file. It is yours and it does not expire.

## The four things people get wrong on the first go

**"I do not know the numbers."** Write down that you do not know. An assistant told *"I do not
track where people come from"* gives you a different and better answer than one told nothing.

**Leaving "what I am not willing to do" blank.** This is the most valuable section and the one
everybody skips. Every generic answer you have ever received was generic because nothing was ruled
out. *"I will not do video"* or *"I have no budget"* or *"I cannot work evenings"* removes half the
useless advice before it is written.

**Filling it in inside the chat window.** Then the conversation ends and it is gone. **Keep it in a
file on your own machine.** That is the difference between doing this once and doing it forever.

**Making it beautiful.** Do not. It is a working document. Ugly and true beats tidy and vague.

---

## Why the last line matters most

> *If something I told you rules an idea out, say so instead of suggesting it.*

Without that sentence, an assistant will happily suggest the thing you just said you will not do.
It is not being difficult. It is retrieving what usually gets said, because that is what it is for.

That sentence is you telling it: **do not retrieve. Answer me.**

## What just happened

You did not learn a prompt trick. You made a record — outside the box, in your own hands, that you
can hand to any assistant, this week or next year.

The box does not remember you. **The file does.**
